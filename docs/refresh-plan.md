# Plan: refresh the published pages and bring them up to current pymrm

Status: AGREED 2026-10-29 (decisions in section 7); step 0 in progress, scoped to CI on
2026-10-29 (section 4). Published numbers follow [`claims-design.md`](claims-design.md). It builds on
[`page-structure-proposal.md`](page-structure-proposal.md), whose format decisions
(section 5 there) stand. It replaces that proposal's migration rule, "existing pages
move when they are next touched", with a deliberate sweep, at the maintainer's
suggestion: the queue is paused and paper-starved, while the expensive part of every
published page (reading the source, extracting the data, provenance, verifying the
claims) is done and guarded by `agreement.json`.

## 1. Why now

- **Extension is blocked on papers.** 140 cases need a paper; no T0/P1 case with a
  source on disk is left (handoff, 2026-08-15).
- **Refactoring under a regression net is cheap.** Every page pins its metrics in
  `agreement.json` and CI compares them. A change that keeps every metric within its
  tolerance cannot have changed a published conclusion.
- **pymrm moved.** 3.0.0 redesigned `newton` (damping, `bounds`, `scale`, a
  backward-error gate, `NewtonBreakdown`), 3.0.1 fixed the bound rule, 3.1.0 added
  `ConvFluxTVD` (TVD fluxes with exact Jacobians), and the library now has
  `{"outflow": True}`, `pseudo_transient`, `continuation` and coupling without
  hand-computed indices (`update_array_indices`, `construct_interface_matrices`).
- **The gallery teaches.** It is the largest public body of pymrm code (82 pages,
  85 000 lines of notebook code, median 980 per page) and a source of the plugin's
  eval cases. Stale idioms here become stale idioms in agents.

## 3. What a refreshed page is

Two lanes, because they need different checks.

### Lane A: format (must not change any number)

Per [`page-structure-proposal.md`](page-structure-proposal.md):
- `model.py` where the page defines a model class (about 50 pages), with docstrings,
  no plotting or data loading, and a seconds-long test (import, one solve, one stored
  value);
- a `jupytext` percent-format `index.py` as the edited source, replacing the string
  generator `build_page.py`;
- a bootstrap that installs the pinned `pymrm` from `meta.yaml` and fetches helpers,
  data and `model.py` from the release tag, never `main`;
- notebook maths that passes `check_notebook_math.py`.

- every published number registered with `gallery_utils.Claims` as a value, bound or
  exact result ([`claims-design.md`](claims-design.md)), prose numbers checked with
  `claims.says`, and a break row that fails each non-structural claim.

**Acceptance:** every metric unchanged within its stored tolerance, or reclassified with
its reason (a value that turns out to be noise becomes a bound); the page passes under
`OPENBLAS_CORETYPE` Haswell, Sandybridge and Nehalem; `check_claims.py` and
`check_model.py` report nothing new; the notebook runs from a clean download.
Pages that are closed forms or correlation audits (many in A1, A4, H, J) get the
bootstrap, pin and maths fixes, and no `model.py`.

### Lane B: numerics (may change numbers)

Each item is done only where it removes a workaround or makes the code read as current
pymrm. Modernising for its own sake is not a reason.

| Change | Pages | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `clip_approach` to `bounds=` | `B1.3`, `F3.2` | completes the 2026-10-22 batch; then newton plan decision 4 (deprecate `clip_approach`?) can be taken |
| Deferred-correction TVD to `ConvFluxTVD` | Newton-solved: `A2.4 A2.8 A3.8 B1.7` | P10: steady deferred correction converges slowly or stalls; use `damping="monotone"` |
| | transient, no `newton`: `A2.1 A2.5 A2.6 A3.15 B2.2 B2.3 I1.3 J1.10` | lagging the correction per time step is a time-discretisation choice, not the P10 stall; judge per page |
| Outflow workaround to `{"outflow": True}` | `A2.4` | keep the old construction as an identity row: the page then proves the library bc equals its own derivation |
| Own pseudo-time and continuation to `pseudo_transient` / `continuation` | the 9 and the 21 above | reported features stay located on deterministic references; `continuation` rejects a jumping corrector, which may move a branch |
| Remove the obsolete `solver=` pin | `D1.1` | the 3.0.0 default is direct LU |
| Hand indices to the coupling API | `A2.5`, `B3.2` | also `B3.2`'s `sp.diags` stencil |
| Exception handling around solves | 16 pages | breakdowns raise `NewtonBreakdown`, non-convergence is returned: check none now reads a returned failure as success |

**Acceptance:** metrics unchanged, or every changed metric listed with its old and new
value, explained on the page, and re-checked by a verifier scoped to what changed. The
`D1.1` lesson applies: scope the check at what the fixer added.

## 4. Order

0. **CI green.** Build `Claims`, agreement format 2, the pinned arithmetic, the lock
   and the CI jobs (`claims-design.md`). Migrate only the pages that fail CI: the six
   that failed on other machines (`D1.1 F3.2 J4.1 J4.6 J4.8 J5.3`) and the three whose
   metrics moved in the locked environment (`B2.3 J1.1 J1.10`). The other pages whose
   metrics move between kernels (about 20, `docs/reproducibility/spread.json`) are
   migrated in lane A when the sweep reaches them, so each page is touched once; until
   then the weekly portability job reports on them without blocking.
1. **Infrastructure, one PR.** Calendar tag in the publish workflow; the bootstrap
   that loads `model.py` and fetches from the tag; the pin read from `meta.yaml`;
   `jupytext` configuration; a CI job for the model tests; `check_model.py` and
   `check_notebook_math.py` in CI, warning first.
2. **Pilot on three pages,** chosen to cover both lanes and three structures:
   - `A2.4` tanks in series: outflow bc, TVD, transient, small;
   - `B1.1` Thiele/Weisz–Hicks: pellet, `NumJac`, `bounds`, multiplicity and continuation;
   - one reactor model others will reuse, `D2.2` or `C2.10`.

   Measure the cost per page in each lane, then decide whether the sweep is worth it.
3. **Sweep by reuse value:** reactor and multi-domain models (S7, S8; sections D, G, H,
   I) first, transport pages (A2, A3, B) next, closed-form pages last. Closed-form
   pages are lane A only and can be batched by section. `D1.1` (4500 s, a delicate
   continuation ladder) goes last and gets lane A only unless a finding forces more.
4. **One page per commit.** Lane B changes that alter a metric are verified one case
   at a time, the rule of 2026-08-07. Lane A edits are checked mechanically and can
   share a working session per section.

## 5. Learnings for the pymrm plugin

Every page refresh records what it taught in
[`refresh-learnings.md`](refresh-learnings.md), one row per finding. A finding
qualifies when it would change what an agent writes or checks, and it carries
evidence (page, numbers). Each row names a target in the pymrm repo: a pitfall with
its test (`conventions/references/pitfalls.md`), the API map, the style guide, an
exemplar, a new eval case built from the page, or the library itself (a bug or a
missing API). Rows are flushed to pymrm as one PR per batch of about ten pages, and
the row records the PR.

This also runs in the other direction: a refreshed `model.py` that is clean, tested
and public is a candidate plugin exemplar.

## 6. Effort

Unknown until the pilot measures it. What is known:
- Building a page cost about 11 to 12 M weighted tokens all-in, mostly source reading
  and verification passes. None of that is repeated here.
- Lane A is mechanical with a pass/fail check (metrics, model test, maths checker). It
  suits `implement` on Sonnet with a complete brief per page.
- Lane B costs a verifier pass whenever a metric moves. The pilot shows how often
  that happens.
- `D1.1`, `J4.1`, `J4.6` take minutes to over an hour per run, which bounds how fast
  any change to them can be checked.

## 7. Decisions (agreed with the maintainer on 2026-10-29)

1. **Sweep** the published pages instead of migrating them when touched, after step 0
   and the pilot.
2. **Lane B may change a published number,** provided the change and its reason are
   shown on the page and verified.
3. **Deprecate `clip_approach`** in pymrm once `B1.3` and `F3.2` no longer use it
   (newton plan, decision 4).
