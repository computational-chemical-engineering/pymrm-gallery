# B1.4 — The Weisz–Prater criterion

**Catalog ID:** `B1.4` · **Section:** B · **Tier:** T0 · **Structures:** `S3`
· **Provenance tier:** 6 (no measurement anywhere in either source)

The inverse of every other pellet page in the gallery. `B1.1`/`B1.5` start from a
known rate constant and compute the effectiveness factor; the Weisz–Prater
criterion asks what an experimenter can conclude from **observables alone** —
measured rate, particle size, effective diffusivity, surface concentration —
without knowing the kinetics:

```
Phi = (dN/dt) * (1/c_0) * (R^2/D)  <  1
```

## Provenance — the reprint route

**Weisz & Prater (1954), *Adv. Catal.* 6, 143 is not on disk** and has no
open-access route; it is a pre-DOI book chapter. It is cited as the origin, from
Weisz & Hicks' own reference list, and **was not consulted**.

Everything transcribed is from **Weisz & Hicks (1962)**, *Chem. Eng. Sci.*
**17**(4), 265–275, `doi:10.1016/0009-2509(62)85005-2`, which is on disk at
`~/papers/pymrm-gallery/1-s2.0-0009250962850052-main.pdf` and is already the
source of `B1.1`/`B1.5` and `B1.6`. It carries the case comfortably: it prints
the criterion as **eq. (1)**, states its purpose, gives the observable group as
**eq. (11)**, extends it to the exothermic case as **eq. (10a)**, devotes its
**Section V** to the observable formulation, and tests it against its own Fig. 2.

An attribution point the page states plainly: Weisz & Hicks attach eq. (1) not
to their reference [5] (Weisz & Prater 1954) but to **[9] Weisz, *Z. Phys.
Chem.* 1957 **11** 1**. Reference [5] is named and used in the same paper, for
the shape-insensitivity of η(φ) and for the printed Q′/Q relation. Both are
recorded; neither was consulted.

Every equation was read off 600 dpi renders (journal pages 265, 267, 268, 269,
272, 273); both references above come from the literature-cited block on page
274, which prints **no chapter title** for [5], so the page gives none. The
scan's text layer is unusable for equations — eq. (1) OCRs as `$:$<I` and
eq. (10a) as `expCrBK1 + LOI`.

## What the page does

1. **What Φ < 1 actually guarantees**, computed exactly rather than asserted at
   order of magnitude — across three geometries, two length-scale conventions
   and five rate laws.
2. **A false-negative map** over φ₀, β and γ: what each criterion *would have
   concluded*, against the truth from the forward solution. The isothermal form
   against Weisz & Hicks' own non-isothermal extension, eq. (10a), including
   the endothermic case where the extension inverts.
3. **A test of the paper's Section V claim** that the observable "has removed
   the multiplicity … a given observed rate defines the system uniquely". It
   postpones the multiplicity by a factor of two to four in βγ; it does not
   remove it. **Every fold sits below Φ = 1** — inside the band the criterion
   certifies as safe — so eq. (1) keeps a single verdict boundary but a *pass*
   stops meaning anything definite: all 14 ambiguous measurements on the page's
   grid have Φ < 1, and the worst clears the threshold threefold while admitting
   η = 2.3, 5.1 or 226. The boundary is located against the β labels Fig. 7
   actually carries (0.8, 0.8, 0.4, 0.3 for its four γ panels, read off a 600 dpi
   render of page 272, not digitised): one of the four panels contains a drawn
   curve in the folded regime, and whether the printed figure could have shown it
   is measured in pixels rather than asserted — the fold spans ≈ 7 px on that
   render against a 6–7 px printed stroke, so the figure is *silent*, neither
   showing the fold nor ruling it out. A limit on the claim, not a refutation of
   the paper.

## Validation

No experiment — tier 6, and the page says so repeatedly. Ranked:

1. the shooting reference against the exact isothermal η, three geometries;
2. pymrm against the same closed form, refined, at the observed order;
3. **pymrm against the shooting solution in the non-isothermal case**, over the
   whole branch including its unstable part — two methods sharing no code, and
   the page's real discretisation test;
4. the Φ = 1 threshold found twice, by bisection on pymrm and on the closed form;
5. two printed numerical statements reproduced and used as inputs to nothing:
   the worked γ = 20, β = 0.3 case (`φ₀ ~ 0.1`) and the multiplicity onset at
   `βγ ≳ 5`; plus the paper's claim that Q′/Q lies between 1 and ½;
6. **five injected defects**, with the table of what moves and what does not;
7. the flux/volume form of eq. (11), reported as the structural identity it is.
