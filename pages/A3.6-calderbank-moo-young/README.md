## What the page shows

Two correlations for the continuous-phase mass-transfer coefficient in a
dispersion whose particles move freely under gravity, differing only in the
Schmidt exponent — $2/3$ for rigid interfaces (hindered flow, Froessling) and
$1/2$ for mobile ones (unhindered flow, Higbie). The page:

1. **reproduces the paper's own two theoretical derivations** of eq. (1)'s
   constant — 0.276000 against a printed 0.28 (Froessling + Allen) and 0.339599
   against a printed 0.34 (Friedlander + Stokes), neither fitted;
2. **rebuilds the exponent split in pymrm** as a concentration boundary layer
   over an interface that is held still or allowed to move, checked against the
   Lévêque and Higbie closed forms to $8\times10^{-6}$ extrapolated;
3. **maps the transition** the paper describes and never models: one group
   $\Lambda = u_s/(\dot\gamma\,\delta_{\rm Lev})$ controls it, halfway at
   $\Lambda = 0.2721$;
4. **reports two places where the paper's own arithmetic does not close** —
   eq. (6) cannot produce eq. (7), and eq. (9) does not meet Chilton–Colburn at
   $N_{Re}=10{,}000$ nor within ±12 % — and **corrects a printed table
   exponent** from the paper's own correlation, with a control that passes.

## What this page does NOT do

- **It does not judge 0.31, 0.42 or 0.13.** Those are regression lines through
  Figs 1, 2 and 6. The evidence about them is the scatter in those figures, no
  figure is digitised here (the route needs a maintainer review and none is
  available), and the page says so instead of substituting something that looks
  like an answer.
- **The corner evaluations of eqs. (1) and (2) against the Range of Variables
  table are a goodness of fit, not a validation** — those constants were fitted
  to the very runs whose $k_L$ ranges they are compared against. Labelled as such
  in the notebook, in `meta.yaml`, in `models_entry.yaml` and in the CSV sidecar.
- **The boundary layer does not predict the constants.** It reproduces the
  exponents and the interpolation, which is the mechanism claim; 0.31 and 0.42
  absorb bubble shape, slip velocity and swarm hindrance, none of which a flat
  interface contains. Its two pure limits are fixed by dimensional analysis, so
  the exponents test the implementation rather than the physics — stated where
  the numbers are printed.
- **It shares a limit with `A3.3`.** The mobile branch reduces to the same
  penetration problem Danckwerts' page solves; the two are not independent
  confirmations of each other, and this page checks against the closed form, not
  against `A3.3`'s output. No number, dataset or sentence from `A3.1`, `A3.3` or
  `A3.4` is reused here.
