# B1.7 — The Mears criteria

**Catalog ID:** `B1.7` · **Section:** B · **Tier:** T0 · **Structures:** `S3` + `S4`
· **Provenance tier:** 6 (no measurement, figure or table anywhere in the source)

The experimenter's side of the pellet problem, completing the arc of
`B1.1`/`B1.4`/`B1.6`: the one-line inequalities in **observables** that decide
whether a measured rate is chemistry or transport — intraparticle, interphase,
interparticle — almost all claiming the same promise, *satisfy this and the
rate deviates by less than 5 %*. This page solves the underlying pellet, film
and dispersion problems exactly and measures what each printed boundary
actually admits.

## Provenance

**Mears, D. E. (1971), "Tests for Transport Limitations in Experimental
Catalytic Reactors", *Ind. Eng. Chem. Process Des. Develop.* 10(4), 541–547,
`doi:10.1021/i260040a020`**, on disk at
`~/papers/pymrm-gallery/Mears1971-tests-for-transport-limitations-IECPDD10-541.pdf`, is the only document consulted.

Two traps the next reader should know:

- **The PDF opens with the tail of the *preceding* article** (a
  catalytic-cracking study discussing "the Voorhies (1945) relationship" — it
  looks like `B2.1`'s paper). The Mears article starts partway down page 1 of
  the file; identity was established from the title block on that page, the
  running head, and the ACS download stamp `article-pdf/10/4/541/`.
- **The text layer destroys exactly the load-bearing symbols**: the
  effectiveness factor extracts as a bare comma, eq. 23's `0.4` as `°'4`,
  eq. 25's `5.3` as `°-`. Every threshold was read off 300 dpi renders — the
  scan's *native* resolution (CCITT-G4 bilevel at 300 ppi), so higher renders
  only interpolate.

An attribution point stated plainly on the page: **this paper is a review**,
and the criteria "derived by the author" are derived in two companion papers —
Mears 1971a (*Chem. Eng. Sci.*, "in press" in the reference list; the axial
criterion) and Mears 1971b (*J. Catal.* **20**, 127; the interphase and
interparticle heat criteria). Neither companion was consulted; the page audits
the criteria exactly as the review states them, which is also how the
literature cites them.

## What the page does

1. **Checks the transcription against the paper's own internal identities** —
   0.22 = 1/√20, "four and a half times" = √20, Carberry's 0.1 = 2 × 0.15,
   eq. 15's Biot threshold 10 = 2 × 0.75/0.15 — and measures the perturbation
   slope n/15 that makes 0.75 the constant the paper says it rounded to 1.
2. **Audits each criterion at its own printed boundary against exact solves**:
   the deviation admitted, per criterion, per kinetics. One is exact (eq. 17
   at first order), the intraparticle family is liberal by exactly its own
   rounding (6.3–6.8 % at the printed bounds), eq. 9 has a measured window of
   no power around the compensation point, eq. 21 fails structurally at low
   Biot number, and Mears' axial criterion holds everywhere tested.
3. **Measures what stacking tolerances costs**: passing eqs. 1, 17 and 21
   simultaneously still admits ≈11 %, at an interior optimum Bi_m ≈ 6 where
   two criteria bind at once. (The first staged version said 9.0 % — a
   Bi_m-sweep artifact caught in verification and kept as a break row.)
4. **Places `B1.4`'s fold finding**: the multivalued observable that defeats
   eq. 1 (certifying η ≈ 3.1e3) does not defeat eq. 9, whose bound has already
   shrunk below the ignited branch's reach — its band edge admits 7.2 %, a
   root-found supremum (the first staged version's 5.7 % was a grid max,
   caught in verification and kept as a break row).
